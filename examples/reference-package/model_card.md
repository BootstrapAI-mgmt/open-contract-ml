# Contract Reference -- Brake Disc TMF Life

> The worked conformant model card for Contract v1. Its eleven H2 sections
> are the reconciled required set (spec section 4.2). Every number below is
> measured and appears identically in `validation_report.json`.

## TL;DR

Predicts brake-disc thermomechanical fatigue life in cycles from a peak rotor
temperature, a cycle count and a material designation, with a 90 percent
split-conformal predictive interval. It exists to give the Contract a real
artifact to be validated against; it is a closed-form log-linear fit on a
synthetic corpus, not an engineering surrogate.

## Intended use

- Exercising a contract consumer -- registry, dispatcher, conformance checker --
  against a package known to be conformant.
- Demonstrating the required section set, the provenance block and the tiered
  validation report in one place.
- Serving as the diff target when a producer repo is brought onto the contract:
  what physics-surrogates and this repo must additionally emit is exactly the
  delta against this package.

## Out of scope

- Any engineering or design decision. The corpus is synthetic; the fit carries
  no physical validation and no rung-4 anchor.
- Materials outside GG20 / GG25 / GGG70, which the entrypoint refuses by name.
- Inputs outside the declared ranges: the OOD guard is an axis-aligned box on
  the manifest's own `range` fields and refuses anything outside them.
- Substituting for the field lanes. This package is `scalar_in_scalar_out`; the
  physics tiers B3, B5 and B6 are NOT_APPLICABLE here by construction.

## Training data

- **Source:** synthetic corpus generated by
  `.agent-scratch/build_reference_package.py`, seeded (`default_rng(0)`), from a
  closed-form log-life law plus Gaussian noise of 0.12 in log space.
- **Size:** 600 designs; 324 train / 108 calibration /
  108 interior test / 60 corner (extrapolation).
- **Distribution:** `peak_temp_K` uniform on [473.15, 1123.15];
  `cycle_count` log-uniform on [10, 100000]; `material` uniform over the three
  designations.
- **Split policy:** the corner split is every design at or above the 90th
  percentile of `cycle_count` (30,238 cycles). It is disjoint from
  train, calibration and interior test, so A2 measures real extrapolation.
- **Known bias:** the corpus is drawn from the same law the model fits, so A1
  flatters the architecture. This is stated rather than hidden -- it is why the
  package claims no validation anchor in C4.

## Architecture

Closed-form log-linear least squares (`numpy.linalg.lstsq`) on five columns:
intercept, `peak_temp_K`, `log(cycle_count)`, and two one-hot columns for GG20
and GGG70 with GG25 as the reference level. Prediction is
`exp(intercept + b_T * peak_temp_K + b_C * log(cycle_count) + offset[material])`.
There is no iteration, no random initialisation and no learned nonlinearity, so
a refit on the same rows reproduces the coefficients bitwise. The whole model is
the 6 floats in `model_weights.json`.

## Training configuration

| Element | Value |
|---|---|
| Estimator | `numpy.linalg.lstsq`, `rcond=None` |
| Iterations | none (closed form) |
| Regularisation | none |
| Seeds | corpus `default_rng(0)`; split permutation `default_rng(1)` |
| Coefficient rounding | 6 significant decimals, written to `model_weights.json` |
| Post-rounding re-measurement | every metric below is measured against the *rounded* weights the entrypoint actually loads, not the full-precision fit |
| Calibration | split conformal at alpha = 0.10 on the calibration split |

The rounding step matters: writing rounded coefficients and then re-measuring is
what keeps the card's numbers and the shipped artifact in agreement.

## Performance

Measured on the interior test split (n = 108), in log-life space, against
the rounded shipped weights.

| Slice | n | R2 | RMSE (log) | MAE (log) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interior test | 108 | 0.996077 | 0.122444 | 0.099684 |
| Corner (extrapolation) | 60 | 0.996099 | 0.106891 | 0.080485 |

Degradation ratio corner/interior RMSE = 0.873 against a cap of 3.0.
RMSE ratio against the training-mean predictor = 0.0624 against a cap of
0.5 (A4).

## Uncertainty quantification

Split-conformal predictive intervals at a nominal level of 90 percent. The
nonconformity score is the absolute residual in log space; the quantile is
fitted on the 108-row calibration split with the finite-sample correction
and never on training or test rows. The resulting half-width is
0.210168 in log space, applied symmetrically about the point estimate and
exponentiated to give `life_cycles_lower` / `life_cycles_upper`.

Empirical coverage on the interior test split is 0.9259 against the accepted
band [0.85, 0.96]. Coverage is *not* measured on the corner split and no claim
is made there -- an extrapolation band calibrated on interior rows is not valid
outside them, and the contract records that as an absent measurement rather than an
optimistic one.

## Known failure modes

- **Flattering corpus.** The generator and the estimator share a functional
  form. Real data will not be log-linear, and A1's R2 of 0.996077 says nothing about
  a real rotor.
- **No aleatoric split.** The conformal band absorbs corpus noise and model
  error together; it cannot separate them, so it will not narrow with more data
  the way an honest epistemic band would.
- **Corner coverage unmeasured.** See the UQ section. Treat any prediction above
  30,238 cycles as uncalibrated.
- **Material extrapolation refused, not approximated.** An unknown material is
  an in-band error from the entrypoint, not a nearest-neighbour guess. A
  consumer that ignores the `error` key will read a missing `outputs` key.
- **Bounds hold by construction, not by validation.** B2 passes because `exp()`
  is positive, which is a property of the link function rather than evidence
  that the model respects physics.
